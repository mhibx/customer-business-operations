## 01 — Overview

This demonstration presents a structured approach to customer service and CRM operations in a gaming and digital top-up business context.

The scope covers the customer journey before, during, and after a transaction, including:

- Customer communication
- Order and transaction handling
- Customer issue resolution
- Follow-up
- Customer retention
- Transaction monitoring
- Troubleshooting
- Customer data management
- Complaint handling
- Recurring issue analysis
- Customer experience improvement

The objective is to demonstrate how customer-facing issues can be handled systematically rather than treated only as individual chat conversations.

> **Portfolio classification:** Hands-on demonstration.
>
> The scenarios, ticket records, metrics, and datasets in this project are dummy/simulated and are not presented as confidential company records or verified business results.

## 02 — Customer Journey

```text
Customer Discovery
        ↓
Product / Price Inquiry
        ↓
Order & Payment
        ↓
Transaction Processing
        ↓
Issue Resolution
        ↓
Follow-up
        ↓
Repeat Purchase
```

The customer-service focus changes across the journey: clear information before purchase, transaction verification during purchase, and issue resolution/follow-up after purchase.

See: [`customer-journey.md`](customer-journey.md)

## 03 — Case Studies

Three transaction-support scenarios are included:

1. **Gaming top-up not received**
2. **Wrong game/player ID**
3. **Digital token not received**

The investigation approach focuses on verifying available transaction information before deciding whether a case should be resolved, monitored, escalated, refunded, or reprocessed according to the applicable procedure.

See: [`case-studies.md`](case-studies.md)

## 04 — Complaint Handling

A structured complaint-handling approach is demonstrated through:

```text
Acknowledge
    ↓
Clarify
    ↓
Investigate
    ↓
Update
    ↓
Resolve / Escalate
    ↓
Follow-up
```

A key principle is to avoid making the customer repeatedly provide information that has already been collected.
