# Ticket & Transaction Management

## Purpose

Customer issues should be recorded in a structured way so they can be tracked, resolved, and analyzed.

A ticket should not exist only as a chat conversation.

## Example Ticket Dataset

| Ticket | Issue | Category | Priority | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-001 | Diamond belum masuk | Top-Up | High | Resolved |
| CS-002 | Salah Player ID | Top-Up | Medium | Investigating |
| CS-003 | Token belum diterima | Token | High | Resolved |
| CS-004 | Tanya harga produk | Inquiry | Low | Resolved |
| CS-005 | Pembayaran pending | Payment | High | Escalated |

> The records above are dummy examples for portfolio demonstration.

## Information Recorded

A support ticket can contain:

- Customer
- Transaction ID
- Product
- Issue
- Time reported
- Status
- Action taken
- Resolution
- Follow-up

## Example Ticket Lifecycle

```text
New
 ↓
Classified
 ↓
Investigating
 ↓
Resolved / Escalated
 ↓
Follow-up
 ↓
Closed
```

## Why Ticket Management Matters

Structured records make it possible to move from individual issue handling to operational analysis.

```text
Individual Tickets
       ↓
Issue Categories
       ↓
Recurring Issues
       ↓
Root-Cause Analysis
       ↓
Process Improvement
```

This connects Customer Service activity with CRM and operational improvement.
